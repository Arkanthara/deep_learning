## Understand the problem

Dot-product attention separates three roles: a query Q expresses what is sought, keys K determine compatibility, and values V provide the information aggregated. With T queries and S keys, the score matrix has shape T × S. Softmax must normalise over the S keys for each query.

## How it works

The √dₖ factor limits dot-product scale as dimension grows. In self-attention, Q, K and V come from the same sequence through learned projections; in cross-attention, queries come from one sequence and keys/values from another. A causal mask excludes the future. Self-attention without positional information is permutation equivariant: it routes content but does not inherently know order. Positional encodings make order accessible.

$$
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)=\operatorname{softmax}_{row}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\right)V
$$

## Develop the reasoning

If q and k components are independent, centred and unit-variance, their dot product has variance dₖ. Dividing by √dₖ brings scale back towards unit variance and limits excessively saturated softmax at initialisation. A mask excludes a key by assigning −∞ before softmax, not merely setting its score to 0. In self-attention without positions or an order-encoding mask, permuting inputs permutes outputs; content alone therefore cannot distinguish sentences with the same words in different orders.

## Worked example

Q has shape (5,8), K is (7,8), and V is (7,16). QKᵀ gives (5,7), softmax keeps (5,7), and AV gives (5,16). Value dimension may therefore differ from key dimension.

## Read the code

Run this example in a Python environment with PyTorch. In-browser execution is planned for V2.

```python
import math
import torch

torch.manual_seed(7)
Q, K, V = (torch.randn(1, 5, 8) for _ in range(3))
scores = Q @ K.transpose(-2, -1) / math.sqrt(Q.size(-1))
future = torch.ones(5, 5, dtype=torch.bool).triu(diagonal=1)
scores = scores.masked_fill(future, float("-inf"))
attention = scores.softmax(dim=-1)
output = attention @ V
print(attention.sum(dim=-1))  # each row sums to 1
print(output.shape)  # (1, 5, 8)
```

## Understanding check

Why is setting a forbidden score to 0 insufficient to mask it?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

exp(0)=1, so the key would still receive positive mass after normalisation. With −∞ its exponential is 0 and its contribution disappears, provided another key is allowed.

</details>
