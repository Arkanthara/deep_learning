## Understand the problem

Autograd records operations performed on tensors requiring gradients. Starting from a scalar loss, backward computes derivatives and accumulates them in the grad attributes of relevant leaf tensors. The graph follows the computation actually executed.

## How it works

Gradients accumulate: before a new independent update, clear them. detach severs a graph dependency; no_grad prevents recording. For a vector output, backpropagation requires an output sensitivity and computes a vector–Jacobian product, not automatically the full Jacobian.

$$
x=3,\quad y=x^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{dy}{dx}=2x=6
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Automatic differentiation applies known local derivatives, without finite differences or symbolic transformation of the entire expression. For nonscalar output y, providing a vector v differentiates $v^\top y$. `autograd.grad` explicitly returns requested derivatives, while `backward` accumulates leaf gradients. After a backward pass, intermediate values may be freed; recomputing a forward pass is the normal approach. `retain_graph=True` preserves that graph at a memory cost, but does not replace correct reasoning about dependencies.

## Worked example

For x = 3 and y = x², y.backward() gives x.grad = 6. Rebuilding y and calling backward without clearing gradients adds another 6.

## Understanding check

If y=(x²,3x) and supplied sensitivity is v=(2,1), what derivative results at x=4?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

We differentiate 2x²+3x, giving 4x+3=19. This is a vector–Jacobian product, not a separate list of all output derivatives.

</details>
