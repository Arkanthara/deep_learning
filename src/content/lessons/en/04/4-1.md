## Understand the problem

A network need not be a chain of layers. A directed acyclic graph can contain branches, additions and shared parameters. Nodes represent tensor operations; edges indicate which values feed which operations.

## How it works

The forward pass follows a topological order. The backward pass follows the reverse order and adds every contribution reaching a reused value. Sharing a parameter does not create independent parameters: contributions accumulate into the same gradient.

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=\sum_{v\in\mathrm{children}(x)}\frac{\partial L}{\partial v}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

The local rule must account for every use of a value. If u=f(x), v=g(u), w=h(u), and L=q(v,w), u’s gradient receives contributions from both v and w. This accumulation differentiates residual connections, shared weights and multiple losses. An acyclic graph admits a computation order without circular dependencies; a recurrent network is handled by unrolling a finite number of time steps into such a graph.

## Worked example

If y = x² + 3x, both branches depend on x. At x = 2, their derivatives are 4 and 3; the total derivative is 7.

## Understanding check

For L=(wx)²+(wy)², with x=1, y=2 and w=3, compute dL/dw.

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

The branches give 2wx²=6 and 2wy²=24. Because they share the same w, contributions add: dL/dw=30.

</details>
