## Understand the problem

An autoencoder learns to reconstruct its input. Encoder f produces a latent representation z and decoder g reconstructs x from z. A bottleneck or another constraint forces the model to retain useful structure rather than trivially copying the input.

## How it works

Squared loss measures reconstruction error. Unlike PCA, nonlinear networks can represent curved and complex variations. However, good reconstruction does not automatically organise latent codes into a simple distribution. Generating with an arbitrary Gaussian z can land in a region never visited during training.

$$
\min_{f,g}\mathbb E_X\left[\|X-g(f(X))\|_2^2\right]
$$

## Develop the reasoning

A linear autoencoder with squared loss and a k-dimensional bottleneck can recover the principal subspace, without its coordinates necessarily being canonical PCA vectors. Nonlinearity extends this family to curved manifolds. Without constraints, identity trivially minimises reconstruction and imposes no abstraction. Synthesis additionally requires choosing a z distribution matching encoded codes. Fitting a simple Gaussian afterwards can ignore holes, curvature and modes; a VAE addresses this within its objective.

## Worked example

Encoding a 784-value image into 8 coordinates gives strong compression. Interpolating between two codes may produce an interesting transition, but does not prove that every latent location yields a valid image.

## Understanding check

Why might interpolation between two observed codes fail even if both reconstruct well?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

The segment may cross a latent region where the encoder places almost no data. The decoder is weakly constrained there and may produce an incoherent output.

</details>
