## Understand the problem

A conditional GAN receives information y, such as a class or source image. The generator learns G(z,y); the discriminator also receives y to check output consistency with that condition. The goal is to model a distribution of possible outputs for a given condition.

## How it works

Pix2pix learns from aligned image pairs, combining adversarial loss with an L1 penalty. A patch discriminator assesses local structure; a U-Net-like generator preserves details through cross-resolution connections. CycleGAN handles unpaired collections using two translations and a return constraint: F(G(x)) should reconstruct x. This constraint reduces ambiguity without identifying a unique semantically correct translation.

$$
\mathcal L_{cyc}=\mathbb E_x\|F(G(x))-x\|_1+\mathbb E_y\|G(F(y))-y\|_1
$$

## Develop the reasoning

If only G receives the condition and D does not, D can reward a realistic image without checking it matches the request. Conditioning D lets it judge the pair. In pix2pix, L1 encourages global fidelity while local discrimination encourages plausible detail. In CycleGAN the cycle does not impose a known pairing: multiple bijections between domains can satisfy round-trip consistency. Visual quality and preservation of relevant information must therefore be evaluated separately.

## Worked example

Translating edges into shoes with pix2pix requires corresponding photographs. Transforming horse and zebra collections with CycleGAN does not require each horse to have a paired zebra.

## Understanding check

A generator asked for class “7” always produces realistic “3” digits. Why can an unconditional discriminator miss this defect?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

It only judges whether the image looks real, without knowing the requested class. The image–condition pair is required to assess consistency.

</details>
