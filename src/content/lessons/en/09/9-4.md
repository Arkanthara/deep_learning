## Understand the problem

Keeping weights fixed and optimising pixels can find an input that strongly activates a unit. The gradient now updates the image. Unconstrained maximisation often favours unrealistic textures or high frequencies because a classifier is not a complete model of the image distribution.

## How it works

A smoothness penalty, for example on changes at multiple scales, favours more readable structures. The same principle explains adversarial examples: maximise classification loss under a small-perturbation constraint. A gradient-selected direction can be much more damaging than random noise of equal norm. Constraints and preprocessing determine the problem actually optimised.

$$
x^*=\arg\max_x\bigl[s_c(x)-\lambda\Omega(x)\bigr]
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Activation maximisation performs gradient ascent on inputs, whereas ordinary learning minimises a loss over parameters. An untargeted attack increases the correct-label loss; a targeted attack favours a chosen class. To keep perturbations admissible, each step may be projected into a ball around the input and into the valid pixel range. The network must stay fixed with controlled mode, otherwise the experiment mixes model changes with signal changes.

## Worked example

To visualise a class, increase its score while penalising an overly irregular image. To induce an error on an existing image, instead increase its true-class loss while limiting distance from the original.
