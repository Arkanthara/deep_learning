## Understand the problem

Parameters carry information acquired during training. In a small linear layer, a weight vector can be linked to a direction or boundary. In an initial convolutional layer, filters can be visualised as small images, revealing colour, orientation or texture selectivity.

## How it works

A deeper filter acts on already transformed channels, not directly on RGB pixels. Displaying its coefficients as a natural image is therefore ambiguous. Moreover, permuting hidden neurons and adapting subsequent layers can preserve the exact function while changing the weight representation. Parameter inspection is chiefly a diagnostic and intuition tool.

$$
w\in\mathbb R^{C_{out}\times C_{in}\times k_H\times k_W}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Filter visualisation needs an explicit colour scale. Normalising each filter independently reveals structure but prevents direct amplitude comparison. An almost-zero filter can then appear as contrasty as an important one. In a deep layer each channel has its own learned meaning; displaying all coefficients on one plane can hide that structure. Combining weights, norm distributions and responses to examples provides a more reliable analysis.

## Worked example

A filter [−1,0,1] responds to local changes rather than constant intensity. In an initial layer this can be read as sensitivity to transitions; in a deeper layer the same pattern concerns learned features.

## Understanding check

Two filters displayed with identical contrast have norms 0.01 and 10. Can their magnitudes be compared from the image alone?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Not if each image was independently normalised. The scale must be known, or both filters displayed with a shared scale.

</details>
