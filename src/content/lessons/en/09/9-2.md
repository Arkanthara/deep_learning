## Understand the problem

An activation is the network’s response to a particular input. Displaying convolutional maps shows where units respond. This complements filter inspection by combining parameters with actual image content. Representations of multiple examples can also be compared at different network depths.

## How it works

Representations often have too many dimensions to plot directly. PCA preserves a linear subspace; t-SNE builds a representation seeking to preserve local probabilistic neighbourhoods. A t-SNE map may suggest separation, but inter-group distances and sizes are not faithful measures of the original space. A hook can capture intermediate outputs without rewriting the whole model.

$$
x\longmapsto h^{(\ell)}(x)\in\mathbb R^D\longmapsto y\in\mathbb R^2
$$

## Develop the reasoning

To analyse a layer, keep a fixed example set and extract one vector per example. For convolution, specify whether the whole map is flattened or spatially averaged: these choices answer different questions. A hook can retain large tensors and their graphs; for inspection alone, detach values and remove the hook afterwards to avoid memory accumulation. Projections may change with initialisation and hyperparameters, so check conclusion stability.

## Worked example

Comparing activations of digits 3 and 8 may show increasing separation across layers. For a sound comparison, use the same examples, document projection settings and inspect multiple observations.

## Understanding check

What does spatially averaging a (C,H,W) map into a (C,) vector discard?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

It retains mean activity per channel but loses response locations. Maps activated at different positions can have exactly the same mean.

</details>
