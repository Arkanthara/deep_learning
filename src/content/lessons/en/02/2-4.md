## Understand the problem

Parameters are learned on the training set. Hyperparameters, such as depth or learning rate, are selected using the validation set. The test set should provide a final estimate on data that did not guide those choices.

## How it works

Inspecting test results and then modifying the method creates an indirect learning loop. Even without test-set gradients, the procedure eventually adapts to it. With scarce data, cross-validation reuses different splits for selection. Learned preprocessing must also be fitted only on training partitions.

$$
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}}\cap\mathcal D_{\mathrm{val}}=\varnothing,\quad\mathcal D_{\mathrm{test}}\cap(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{train}}\cup\mathcal D_{\mathrm{val}})=\varnothing
$$

## Develop the reasoning

The split must reflect actual independence. If several images come from one patient or measurements from one user, random row-wise splitting may put near-duplicates on both sides. Grouped or chronological splitting may be needed. Within cross-validation, every learned step—mean, vocabulary, feature selection—must be refitted on the training fold. The final model can then be trained on development data with fixed choices, consulting the test set only at the end.

## Worked example

To choose among three learning rates, compare validation scores, fix the choice, then evaluate on the test set. Choosing the best rate using test scores would turn the test set into validation.

## Understanding check

You normalise data before splitting training and validation. What is the problem and how do you fix it?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Validation statistics influence learned preprocessing. Split first, estimate statistics on training data, then apply the same statistics to validation and test.

</details>
