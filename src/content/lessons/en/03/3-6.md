## Understand the problem

Backpropagation efficiently differentiates a composition by applying the chain rule in reverse computational order. The forward pass retains required activations. The backward pass transports loss sensitivity from the output to parameters in the earliest layers.

## How it works

For an affine layer followed by an activation, the local activation derivative multiplies the incoming sensitivity. The weight gradient is then an outer product of that sensitivity and the layer input. Transposed weights transport sensitivity to the preceding layer. Computing derivatives and updating weights are separate operations.

$$
\delta^{(\ell)}=\left(W^{(\ell+1)\top}\delta^{(\ell+1)}\right)\odot\sigma^\prime(s^{(\ell)}),\quad\nabla_{W^{(\ell)}}\mathcal L=\delta^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)\top}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

For a layer $s=Wh+b$, $a=\sigma(s)$, call the incoming sensitivity g = $\partial L/\partial a$. Preactivation sensitivity is $\delta=g\odot\sigma^\prime(s)$. Then $\partial L/\partial W=\delta h^\top$, $\partial L/\partial b=\delta$ and $\partial L/\partial h=W^\top\delta$. The outer product has exactly W’s shape. Across a batch, contributions sum or average according to loss reduction. Checking dimensions is an effective way to detect a missing transpose.

## Worked example

For z = wx and L = z², with x = 3 and w = 2: z = 6, dL/dz = 12 and dz/dw = 3. Their product gives dL/dw = 36.

## Understanding check

W has shape (3,2), h shape (2,) and δ shape (3,). What are the gradient shapes for W and h?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

δhᵀ gives (3,2), matching W. Wᵀδ gives (2,), matching h. A gradient with respect to a tensor has that tensor’s shape.

</details>
