## Understand the problem

An MLP alternates affine transformations and nonlinear activations. Each layer transforms the previous representation; the last produces the values required by the task. Width is the number of units in a layer, while depth counts successive transformations.

## How it works

Without nonlinearities, a composition of affine layers remains affine: stacking matrices does not add this representational capacity. Universal approximation shows that a sufficiently wide hidden layer can approximate continuous functions on a compact domain. It guarantees neither reasonable width, easy training nor good generalisation.

$$
h^{(\ell)}=\sigma(W^{(\ell)}h^{(\ell-1)}+b^{(\ell)})
$$

## Develop the reasoning

A combination of shifted ReLUs builds a piecewise-affine function. The term $a\operatorname{ReLU}(x-t)$ adds slope a after threshold t, leaving the earlier slope unchanged. Choosing thresholds and slope changes can approximate a continuous curve on an interval. In higher dimension this becomes a more complex construction of regions. Output activation depends on the task: for CrossEntropyLoss, retain logits rather than automatically adding softmax to the model.

## Worked example

A 2 → 4 → 1 MLP has 2×4 + 4 = 12 parameters in the first layer and 4×1 + 1 = 5 in the second, for 17 total.
