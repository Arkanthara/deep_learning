## Understand the problem

LDA assumes class-conditional Gaussian distributions sharing one covariance matrix. Bayes’ rule converts these conditional densities into class probabilities. With two classes, the log probability ratio is affine in the input: quadratic terms cancel because the covariance is shared.

## How it works

The probability of class 1 becomes a sigmoid applied to a linear score. Logistic regression uses the same predictor form but fits its parameters directly from labels without estimating two Gaussian densities. Class priors influence the bias and therefore the decision boundary.

$$
P(Y=1\mid x)=\sigma(w^\top x+b),\qquad\sigma(s)=\frac1{1+e^{-s}}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

For a Gaussian with mean $\mu_k$ and shared covariance $\Sigma$, log density contains $-\frac12(x-\mu_k)^\top\Sigma^{-1}(x-\mu_k)$. Subtracting class-0 from class-1 log densities cancels the $x^\top\Sigma^{-1}x$ terms. This leaves $w=\Sigma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)$ and a bias containing mean norms and the log prior ratio. A larger prior can therefore favour a class even at similar distances from both means.

## Worked example

If the two means are −1 and +1, with variance 1 and equally likely classes, the log ratio is 2x. At x = 0, each class has probability 0.5.

## Understanding check

What does a larger prior probability for class 1 change when class-conditional densities are fixed?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

It increases the log prior ratio in the bias. The boundary shifts to assign more points to class 1; the direction w is unchanged.

</details>
