## Understand the problem

The perceptron decides using the sign of a weighted sum of inputs and a bias. Its boundary is a hyperplane. For labels ±1, multiplying the label by the score indicates whether the example is correctly classified: a positive value means the correct side of the boundary.

## How it works

On an error, the algorithm adds the input multiplied by its label to the weight vector. The bias can be handled by adding a constant input coordinate. With bounded inputs and linear separation with a strictly positive margin, the number of corrections is finite. This guarantee disappears for nonseparable data.

$$
y_n(w^\top x_n+b)\leq0\ \Rightarrow\ w\leftarrow w+y_nx_n,\quad b\leftarrow b+y_n
$$

## Develop the reasoning

To understand the correction, examine the signed score $y(w^\top x+b)$. Replacing the weight by $w+yx$ adds $y^2\|x\|^2=\|x\|^2$ to this example’s signed score, a positive contribution. The convergence proof tracks progress along a separating direction while controlling weight norm growth. The bound depends on the ratio of data radius to margin. SVM adds a large-margin objective using hinge loss and regularisation.

## Worked example

With w = (0,0), x = (2,1) and y = +1, a correction gives w = (2,1), so w·x = 5. The score for this example moves in the correct direction.

## Understanding check

An example x=(1,−2), y=−1 is misclassified by w=(0,0). Compute the bias-free update.

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

w becomes (−1,2). The score w·x is −5 and the signed score y(w·x) is 5: the example is now on the correct side.
