## Understand the problem

The handout presents applications in vision, games, translation, generation and biology. They illustrate different outputs: a class, an action, a sequence or a synthetic signal. These examples are a historical snapshot from the course, not a ranking of present-day performance.

## How it works

Progress comes from several interacting factors: decades of research, more data, parallel hardware and reproducible software. PyTorch combines a Python interface with efficient numerical operations and automatic differentiation. Training combines data preparation, a model, a loss and an optimiser; architecture alone is insufficient.

$$
x\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times28\times28}\longmapsto f_\theta(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{10}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

To analyse an application, first write its input–output contract. Classification maps a signal to a finite label set; regression produces continuous values; a generative model describes or samples signals. The loss follows from that contract. Next come data and protocol: what signal is available during learning, what will be seen in use and how will success be measured? More computation enables larger experiments but does not repair an ill-defined objective.

## Worked example

For MNIST, the input is a 1 × 28 × 28 image and the output contains ten logits. A classification loss compares these logits with the target digit; the optimiser adjusts filters and linear layers.

## Understanding check

Match these outputs to tasks: ten digit scores, tomorrow’s temperature, a new image from latent noise.
