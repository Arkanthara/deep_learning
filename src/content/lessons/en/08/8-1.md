## Understand the problem

Classification assigns a category to an image. Detection predicts objects and their positions, often boxes. Semantic segmentation assigns a class to each pixel; instance segmentation also distinguishes individual objects within a class. These tasks require different annotations and metrics.

## How it works

Overall accuracy is not the whole picture. For a positive class, recall measures the fraction of actual positives recovered, and precision the fraction of positive predictions that are correct. A ROC curve varies the threshold between true- and false-positive rates. For regions, intersection over union measures overlap; it is used in box and mask evaluation.

$$
\mathrm{IoU}(A,B)=\frac{|A\cap B|}{|A\cup B|},\quad\mathrm{precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\quad\mathrm{recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Metrics answer different error costs. A false positive triggers an unjustified alert; a false negative misses a target. Changing a threshold generally trades precision against recall. Detection also requires matching predicted and true boxes to avoid counting one object repeatedly. Average precision summarises a precision–recall curve under a defined protocol; mAP averages over classes and sometimes IoU thresholds. Score comparisons therefore require the exact evaluation protocol.

## Worked example

If predicted and true masks each cover 100 pixels with 60 shared, their union is 140 and IoU is 60/140 ≈ 0.429. Computing 60/100 would measure something else.

## Understanding check

A detector finds 8 real objects, produces 2 false alarms and misses 4 objects. Compute precision and recall.

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Precision=8/(8+2)=0.8. Recall=8/(8+4)=2/3. The first denominator counts positive predictions; the second counts actual objects.

</details>
