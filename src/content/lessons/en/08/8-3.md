## Understand the problem

Detecting an object requires both recognising its class and estimating its location. A sliding-window method classifies many regions at high computational cost. Networks share convolutional computation and add regression outputs to refine boxes.

## How it works

The course compares region proposals with one-pass detectors such as original YOLO and SSD. The presented YOLO organises predictions on a grid and combines coordinates, object presence and classes in its loss. SSD uses multiple resolutions for different object sizes. Matching predictions to annotations, background/object imbalance and duplicates are part of the problem.

$$
\mathcal L=\lambda_{box}\mathcal L_{box}+\lambda_{obj}\mathcal L_{obj}+\lambda_{cls}\mathcal L_{cls}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

The network must also handle background, which dominates many images. Localisation terms concern predictions assigned to objects; negatives mainly teach absence of objects. Weightings prevent one loss component from dominating merely through example count. At output, several predictions may describe the same object: score- and overlap-based duplicate suppression is a classical choice. Box IoU is size-sensitive: a few pixels of error strongly penalise small objects.

## Worked example

A box may be well located but misclassified, or correctly classified but poorly located. Successful detection requires both, under a defined overlap threshold and matching protocol.

## Understanding check

Why should coordinate loss not be applied identically to cells without objects?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

There is no meaningful target box there. An artificial coordinate target would teach behaviour unrelated to locating objects.
