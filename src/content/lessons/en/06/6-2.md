## Understand the problem

ReLU preserves positive values and zeros negative ones. Its derivative is 1 on the positive side, avoiding the saturation that tanh or sigmoid exhibit for large inputs. It also produces partly zero representations and piecewise-affine networks.

## How it works

A neuron that is always negative receives no gradient through this activation and can become inactive. Leaky ReLU keeps a small negative slope; PReLU learns it. ELU saturates smoothly on the negative side. Maxout selects the maximum of several affine functions, while CReLU separately retains the positive parts of x and −x.

$$
\operatorname{ReLU}(x)=\max(0,x),\quad\operatorname{LeakyReLU}(x)=\max(ax,x),\quad0<a<1
$$

## Develop the reasoning

ReLU’s derivative is 0 for x<0 and 1 for x>0; at zero a subgradient convention is used. A long chain of positive branches does not contract gradients because of activations alone, but weight matrices still amplify or shrink them. Leaky ReLU avoids a fully flat branch. PReLU adds a slope parameter; CReLU doubles dimension by retaining signs separately. The choice affects both learning dynamics and available feature count.

## Worked example

For x = −2, ReLU produces 0; Leaky ReLU with slope 0.1 produces −0.2. The latter’s derivative remains 0.1, allowing a learning signal through.

## Understanding check

What does CReLU(x)=(ReLU(x),ReLU(−x)) produce for x=−3? Can x be recovered?

<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

The output is (0,3). Subtracting the second component from the first recovers −3, so this activation retains sign information.
