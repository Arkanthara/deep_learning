## Understand the problem

Universal approximation does not compare representation cost. Depth composes reusable transformations. In a ReLU network, each unit divides space into affine regions; composing layers can multiply those regions much more efficiently than simply widening a layer.

## How it works

The handout constructs sawtooth functions whose oscillation count grows exponentially with depth, while a shallow network needs many more units to approximate them. This is an expressivity result for particular functions, not a guarantee for every task. More layers also complicate gradient propagation. Initialisation, activations, normalisation and residual connections address this difficulty.

$$
T(x)=2\operatorname{ReLU}(x)-4\operatorname{ReLU}(x-1/2),\quad x\in[0,1]
$$

## Develop the reasoning

The sawtooth construction demonstrates a compositional advantage, not merely many weights. Each layer folds the interval and reuses the preceding pattern. With natural data we also hope to reuse substructures, though this analogy proves nothing for every task. Effective capacity also depends on optimisation, regularisation and data. Overparameterisation and double-descent phenomena do not contradict the bias–variance identity; they contradict an assumption that its terms always vary monotonically.

## Worked example

A triangle function divides [0,1] into two linear pieces. Composing it with itself creates four pieces, then eight. Composition reuses a simple rule to create fine structure.

## Understanding check

Why are “deeper” and “easier to train” not synonyms?
