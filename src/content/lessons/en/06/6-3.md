## Understand the problem

Dropout randomly zeros activations during training. This perturbation limits dependence on a few units and encourages distributed representations. Weights remain present: the intermediate signal is masked, with fresh masks during computation.

## How it works

PyTorch uses inverted dropout: with drop probability p, retained activations are divided by 1−p. Their expectation is thus unchanged. In eval mode, the module becomes the identity. For convolutional maps with strong local correlations, dropping entire channels can be more useful than dropping isolated pixels.

$$
\tilde x=\frac{m}{1-p}x,\quad m\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1-p),\quad\mathbb E[\tilde x]=x
$$

## Develop the reasoning

The random mask also multiplies gradients in the backward pass: a dropped activation transmits no gradient for that example. Expectation preservation concerns the dropout module’s output, not necessarily the whole network after nonlinearities. The subnetwork-ensemble interpretation is useful but standard inference remains an approximation. DropConnect masks weights rather than activations. Dropout rate and placement are hyperparameters to validate, not an automatic improvement.

## Worked example

An activation x = 2 with p = 0.5 becomes 0 or 4 with equal probability. Its mean remains 2. At evaluation it directly stays 2.

## Understanding check

After model.eval(), should activations still be manually multiplied by 1−p with PyTorch dropout?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

No. Inverted dropout already applied the compensation during training. In eval the module is the identity; extra correction would alter the scale.
