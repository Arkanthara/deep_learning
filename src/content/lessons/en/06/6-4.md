## Understand the problem

Batch normalisation centres and rescales activations using statistics computed over a set of values. During training, these statistics depend on the mini-batch, so each example can affect the transformation of the others. Learned γ and β then determine scale and offset.

## How it works

In the usual configuration, evaluation uses running means and variances accumulated during training. BatchNorm2d normalises each channel over batch and spatial axes. LayerNorm normalises features within an example and does not depend on other batch examples. Choosing axes matters as much as the formula.

$$
y=\gamma\frac{x-\mu_{\mathcal B}}{\sqrt{\sigma^2_{\mathcal B}+\epsilon}}+\beta
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Batch mean and variance depend on all normalised inputs. The gradient with respect to x must therefore include direct effects and effects through these statistics. Treating mean and variance as constants during training would give an incorrect gradient. For BatchNorm2d statistics are per channel over N,H,W, while γ and β are shared spatially. Very small batches can make statistics unstable, motivating normalisations that do not depend on the batch.

## Worked example

For channel values 1 and 3, the mean is 2 and the normalisation variance is 1. Ignoring epsilon and taking γ = 1, β = 0, outputs are −1 and +1.

## Understanding check

For a tensor (8,16,10,10), how many γ and β values does BatchNorm2d(16) have?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

16 γ and 16 β values, or 32 learned parameters. In train mode, statistics use 8×10×10 values per channel.

</details>
