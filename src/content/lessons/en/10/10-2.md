## Understand the problem

A causal model prevents future values from influencing a prediction. With convolutions, this requires masks or asymmetric padding. Predicting a value from the strict past also requires excluding the current value, for example by shifting the input one step.

## How it works

This structure computes all conditional distributions of a known sequence in one training pass. Generation remains sequential because new values are not yet known. Dilations enlarge the receptive field without requiring as many layers. The source presents WaveNet and causal image convolutions, where traversal order and blind spots require care.

$$
R=1+\sum_{\ell=1}^L(k_\ell-1)d_\ell\quad\text{for stride }1
$$

## Develop the reasoning

A causality test fixes a prefix and changes only the suffix. Predictions whose allowed context is unchanged must remain identical. This detects unintended symmetric padding, incorrect masks or normalisation mixing future and past. In 2D, masks follow pixel traversal order; stacking naive filters can leave past regions inaccessible. Dilation expands reach but its pattern can leave uneven sampling coverage.

## Worked example

With size-2 kernels, stride 1 and dilations 1, 2, 4, the receptive field spans 1+1+2+4 = 8 positions. Changing a future input must never change logits at an earlier position.
