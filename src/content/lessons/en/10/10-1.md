## Understand the problem

The probability chain rule factorises a joint distribution into successive conditional probabilities. An autoregressive model predicts each component from preceding ones. The chosen order can follow time, words or a traversal of image pixels; it is part of the model.

## How it works

During training, true prefixes are available: maximising likelihood amounts to summing log probabilities of the true components. During generation, sample one component and feed it back into context before continuing. The handout encodes known values and a separate mask to distinguish an actual zero from an unknown value.

$$
p_\theta(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^T p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})
$$

## Develop the reasoning

Taking logarithms converts the probability product into a sum of local terms. It also avoids numerical underflow from multiplying many small probabilities. Each classification term can be learned with cross-entropy. Training prefixes are real; generated prefixes contain model samples, and errors can change future context. Filling missing pixels also requires a generation order compatible with the learned conditioning: an arbitrary known-pixel set is not automatically handled by a fixed factorisation.

## Worked example

For three variables, p(x₁,x₂,x₃) = p(x₁)p(x₂|x₁)p(x₃|x₁,x₂). If these factors are 0.5, 0.8 and 0.25 for a given sequence, its probability is 0.1.
