## Understand the problem

Adding a parameter penalty changes the tradeoff between fit and complexity. L2 smoothly shrinks weights towards zero; L1 favours sparse solutions, with some coefficients exactly zero at the optimum. Their strength is controlled by λ.

## How it works

The course connects L2 with a Gaussian prior and L1 with a Laplace prior. For squared L2, the added gradient is 2λw. L1 is not differentiable at zero; a soft-threshold proximal operator handles this point cleanly. Ordinary descent using sign can oscillate around zero.

$$
\operatorname{prox}_{\eta\lambda\|\cdot\|_1}(u)=\operatorname{sign}(u)\max(|u|-\eta\lambda,0)
$$

## Develop the reasoning

For squared loss with an L2 penalty, the total derivative adds a force proportional to weight magnitude: larger weights are pulled more strongly towards zero. L1 adds constant-magnitude force away from zero. At zero its subgradient is the interval [−λ,λ], able to offset a small loss gradient: this explains exactly zero optimum coefficients. Soft thresholding implements this idea explicitly. Optimal λ depends on loss normalisation, notably sum versus mean.

## Worked example

After a step yielding u = 0.03, a proximal threshold ηλ = 0.05 produces zero. For u = 0.20, it produces 0.15.

## Understanding check

Minimise (w−2)² + λw². What is the solution and what happens as λ grows?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Setting 2(w−2)+2λw to zero gives w=2/(1+λ). The solution approaches zero but is not exactly zero for finite positive λ.

</details>
