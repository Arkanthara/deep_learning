## Understand the problem

Choosing architecture and settings is an experimental problem. The source recommends starting from an established structure and monitoring learning curves. Training loss indicates optimisation progress; validation indicates transfer to new data.

## How it works

A large learning rate can accelerate early progress, then a reduction can allow finer adjustment. Distinguish loss from classification error. Accuracy depends only on score ordering; cross-entropy also depends on confidence. Rising validation loss can therefore coexist with improved accuracy.

$$
\mathrm{accuracy}=\frac1N\sum_n\mathbf1\!\left[\arg\max_k z_{n,k}=y_n\right]
$$

## Develop the reasoning

A curve diagnoses behaviour only with a consistent protocol: fixed validation data, eval mode, correctly aggregated metrics and a record of settings. If training loss stays high, inspect optimisation, capacity and data. If it falls while validation degrades, inspect generalisation, regularisation and early stopping. A scheduler changes learning rate by schedule or validation signal. Specifying a checkpoint-selection metric in advance avoids retrospectively choosing the most flattering experiment.

## Worked example

On 100 examples, correcting two small mistakes improves accuracy. But if another mistake becomes extremely confident, its large loss can raise average cross-entropy.

## Understanding check

Why should mean batch losses be weighted by batch size when the last batch is smaller?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Otherwise a small final batch receives as much weight as a large batch. The correct global mean sums mean_loss×batch_count and divides by total example count.
