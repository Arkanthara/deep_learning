## Understand the problem

Activations and gradients pass through many multiplications. An unsuitable weight scale can make their variance explode or vanish with depth. Initialisation is therefore more than choosing arbitrary small random numbers.

## How it works

Xavier approximately balances forward and backward variances using fan-in and fan-out. For ReLU, He accounts for removing the negative part and typically uses variance 2/fan-in. These arguments rely on approximate independence and centring assumptions. Normalising inputs complements this scale control.

$$
\operatorname{Var}(W)_{\mathrm{Xavier}}\approx\frac{2}{n_{in}+n_{out}},\qquad\operatorname{Var}(W)_{\mathrm{He}}\approx\frac{2}{n_{in}}
$$

## Develop the reasoning

For s=Σwᵢxᵢ with independent centred terms, Var(s)≈n Var(w) Var(x). Preserving scale therefore calls for weight variance proportional to 1/n. For a symmetric distribution, ReLU removes roughly half the second moment, motivating He’s factor 2. The backward argument uses fan-out. Xavier compromises between both constraints. These calculations guide initialisation; actual activation dependencies and evolving weights limit their accuracy during learning.

## Worked example

For a ReLU layer with 100 inputs, He normal initialisation uses variance 0.02 and standard deviation √0.02 ≈ 0.141, not 0.02.

## Understanding check

If fan-in is multiplied by four in He normal initialisation, how does weight standard deviation change?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the worked answer</summary>

Variance is divided by four and standard deviation by two. Confusing these factors would produce the wrong scale.

</details>
